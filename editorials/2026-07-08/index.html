Decolonising tribal research: whose knowledge counts in India's development story?

Amid accelerating ecological degradation, language loss and extractive development, there is a renewed academic and policy call to treat tribal communities not as passive 'objects of study' but as producers of knowledge. The demand for 'indigenous knowledge sovereignty' reframes an old question for India's forest and Fifth Schedule areas: whose knowledge counts when we plan development?

Strategic autonomy in the shadow of hegemony: India's post-RCEP trade paradigm

As US-China tariff wars resurge and West Asia stays volatile in 2026, India's 2019 decision to walk out of the Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (RCEP) — and its continued refusal to re-join — is back under the scanner. The debate is no longer only about trade; it is a test of what 'strategic autonomy' means for a manufacturing economy in a China-centric region.

Governance / Social JusticeRAS Mains · Governance; access to justice & legal aid; vulnerable sections (women prisoners)

Justice behind bars: legal aid, poor prisoners and the gendered gap

India's prisons remain overcrowded with undertrials, and reviews of the legal-aid system keep returning the same verdict: a strong constitutional and statutory framework exists on paper, yet the poorest — and women prisoners especially — still struggle to access effective legal representation. It is a reminder that access to justice is a cornerstone of the rule of law, not a favour to the marginalised.
