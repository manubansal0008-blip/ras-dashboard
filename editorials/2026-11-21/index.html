Health Spending: Where You Live Decides What the Government Spends on You

Why in news: The National Health Accounts estimates for 2023-24 show wide gaps in what state governments spend on health per person — from ₹1,126 in Bihar to ₹16,026 in Sikkim.

Health is a State subject, so most public spending on hospitals, doctors and medicines comes from state budgets. The new estimates show that government health spending reached ₹4.1 lakh crore, or 1.43% of GDP, but it still covers only 43.5% of total health expenditure. The rest is paid largely by households.

  • Government health expenditure 2023-24: ₹4.1 lakh crore (₹2,959 per person)
  • Share of GDP: 1.43%; share of total health expenditure: 43.5%
  • Highest state: Sikkim ₹16,026; lowest: Bihar ₹1,126; UP ₹1,399
  • North-East average ~₹3,501; UT average ~₹7,209; Chandigarh ₹24,187
  • National Health Policy 2017 target: 2.5% of GDP

The gaps follow fiscal capacity. Large, poorer states have more people and less money per person, while small hill states face higher costs of running services across difficult terrain. Spending alone does not measure quality, but where public spending is low, families pay more from their own pockets, and a single hospital stay can push them into debt.

Rajasthan angle: the State passed the Right to Health Act in 2023 and runs a large health insurance scheme (Mukhyamantri Ayushman Arogya Yojana). Insurance pays for hospital stays, but steady spending on primary health centres, doctors in rural areas and free medicines is what reduces costs for families over time.
