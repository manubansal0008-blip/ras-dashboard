One Nation, One Time: Why India Needs Its Own Clock

Why in news: The Legal Metrology (Indian Standard Time) Rules, 2026, notified under Section 52 of the Legal Metrology Act, 2009, make IST the single legal time reference for official and commercial use.

Banks, stock exchanges, power grids and telecom networks synchronise to the microsecond. Most still take time from foreign satellite systems such as GPS. If that signal is jammed or spoofed, trading, payments and grid control can fail together. The new rules make IST, kept by CSIR-NPL, the single legal reference.

  • Notified under Section 52, Legal Metrology Act, 2009
  • Rules apply 180 days after notification
  • IST is maintained by CSIR-National Physical Laboratory, New Delhi
  • Trial of 'White Rabbit' time-transfer at RRSL Bengaluru (July 2026)

Way forward: phased compliance for critical sectors first, regional time-dissemination centres, NavIC as backup, and audits by CERT-In.

Rajasthan angle: the State's large solar parks and the grid that evacuates their power depend on precise synchronisation; a time failure could trip renewable-heavy grids in western Rajasthan.
