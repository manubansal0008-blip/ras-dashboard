New Base, New Lens: Reading India's 7.8% Growth

Why in news: On 1–2 September 2026 MoSPI released the revised National Accounts series with 2022-23 as base year, and the first quarterly estimate on it showed real GDP growth of 7.8% in Q1 FY27.

A base year is the reference year whose prices and economic structure are used to measure 'real' output. India last shifted base in 2015 (to 2011-12). An old base under-counts new sectors such as digital services and gig work, and uses outdated price weights. The new series also brings in fresh price and production indices (PPI, IIP) on the same base.

  • Real GDP Q1 FY27: ₹81.36 lakh crore (+7.8%)
  • Q1 FY26 growth: 6.9%
  • Nominal GDP Q1 FY27: ₹88.27 lakh crore (+10.3%)
  • New base year: 2022-23 (earlier 2011-12)

Way forward: publish a full back-series, adopt a five-year base-revision cycle, and use annual surveys such as ASUSE and PLFS to measure the informal economy directly.

Rajasthan angle: the State's Directorate of Economics & Statistics must shift GSDP to the new base too; district-level ASUSE data can sharpen planning for Rajasthan's large informal MSME and handicraft sector.
