Green on Paper: What the CAG Audit of the Green India Mission Tells Us

Why in news: A CAG performance audit of the Green India Mission found no visible change at about 70% of plantation sites in satellite images, and Parliament's Public Accounts Committee has summoned Environment Ministry officials on 19 November.

The Green India Mission was meant to be India's flagship answer to forest loss and climate change. The audit shows a familiar gap between targets and ground reality. By March 2025, forest cover expansion reached only about 2.4% of its target, and money expected from other schemes arrived at barely a tenth of the planned amount. Saplings were planted in the wrong places and in the wrong way, so survival stayed low.

  • Audit covered 16 States and UTs (CAG Report No. 4 of 2026)
  • No visible change at about 70% of sites in satellite images
  • Forest cover expansion: 34,090 ha against 14 lakh ha (2.43%) by March 2025
  • Forest quality improvement: 1.13 lakh ha against 14 lakh ha
  • Convergence funds: ₹1,149.14 crore against ₹10,600 crore
  • GIM: one of 8 missions of NAPCC (2008); approved in 2014

Way forward: measure trees that survive after three to five years instead of saplings planted; use satellite and drone checks before payments; plant native species suited to each landscape; give Joint Forest Management committees and gram sabhas a share in benefits; and publish site-wise results so citizens can verify them.

Rajasthan angle: Rajasthan has only about 9–10% of its area under forest and tree cover, and much of the State is desert, grassland and the Aravalli hills. Here, protecting oran, gochar and Aravalli land, and planting drought-hardy native trees such as khejri, rohida and ber, matters more than mass planting of fast-growing exotic species.
