A crisis on wheels: when a free-ride scheme meets a debt-ridden transport corporation

Kerala's 'Priyadarshini' free bus-ride scheme for women on State-owned buses, launched months ago, is turning out to be financially unviable — with the already debt-ridden Kerala State Road Transport Corporation (KSRTC) bearing a cost of about Rs 2 crore a day, and private operators threatening to strike. It is a sharp illustration of the tension every State faces: welfare that empowers versus welfare a treasury cannot sustain.

Polity / Digital RightsRAS Mains · Fundamental rights; right to privacy; data protection & the judiciary

The right to be forgotten: balancing privacy against open justice and free speech

A recent Delhi High Court ruling has, for the first time, laid down a coherent framework for the 'right to be forgotten' in India — the right to have information erased or de-indexed from the digital environment when its continued accessibility is harmful and serves no public interest. The judgment recognises the right as part of the fundamental right to privacy under Article 21, while trying to balance it against open justice and free speech.

Trading blows amid talks: the US, Iran and the fragile future of the Strait of Hormuz

The US President has declared the interim ceasefire with Iran 'over' even as he leaves room for negotiations — a familiar cycle of escalation that threatens the shaky truce and keeps the Strait of Hormuz on edge. With Iran striking US targets in Bahrain and Kuwait and US forces hitting Iranian targets, the world's most important oil chokepoint has become both a bargaining chip and a flashpoint.
