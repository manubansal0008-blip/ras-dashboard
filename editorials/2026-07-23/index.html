Polity & GovernanceRAS Mains · Indian Polity, Fundamental Rights, Civil Society, Internal Security

Tear Gas at the Gates: When the Republic Stops Listening to Its Young

On July 20, largely young demonstrators marching towards Parliament were chased, tear-gassed and baton-charged, with some allegedly hit by pellet guns, while mobile Internet was switched off and Metro stations were shut across the area. A meeting with the protesters was conceded through a Union Minister only after thousands had reached Parliament's doorstep — too late to prevent the confrontation.

In the light of recent youth protests in Delhi, examine the constitutional limits on the state's response to peaceful assembly and suggest measures to rebuild trust between young citizens and the government.

Leaked Papers, Leaking Future: The Real Education Emergency

Protesters at Jantar Mantar demand the Education Minister's resignation and accountability for the NEET paper leak, and the government promises strict action and a successful re-conduct of the exam. But the stand-off is drawing attention away from the larger question: India's chronic under-investment in the education of a very young population.

Death in the Deep: What the Teesta Tunnel Buried Along With Its Workers

Workers in an under-construction tunnel at NHPC's Teesta Stage-VI hydroelectric project in Sikkim's Namchi district struck a pocket of suspected trapped methane. The resulting burst partially damaged the tunnel and killed workers, with rescue operations still under way and the toll rising. The NHPC has announced a detailed investigation.

International Relations & SecurityRAS Mains · International Relations, Maritime Security, West Asia, Indian Ocean Region

Choked at the Gate of Tears: The Narrow Strait Holding a Third of India's Trade

On the heels of the Strait of Hormuz crisis, Iran has reportedly instructed the Houthis to close the Strait of Bab el-Mandeb — between Yemen and the Horn of Africa — if the United States attacks Iran's power plants. Whether or not the Houthis have that capacity, the threat represents a risk to roughly one-third of India's entire trade.

Science-Tech & EconomyRAS Mains · Science & Technology, Artificial Intelligence, Infrastructure, Energy-Water Nexus, Regulation

Thirsty Giants: The AI Boom Drinking India's Water and Power

On July 14, New York became the first US state to halt construction of large new data centres with a one-year moratorium, citing power costs, water supplies and burdened communities. Amsterdam, the Netherlands, Ireland's grid operator and Singapore have all restricted data-centre growth. In India, by contrast, there is hardly any critical political discourse on responsible data-centre development — states compete to subsidise them.

AI data-centre market ($5.5 bn → $90 bn; 12-14 GW by 2035)20% of world's data vs ~3% capacityEnergy-water nexus / groundwater stressState subsidies (UP 2021, TN, Telangana)Ghost infrastructure / AI bubble riskRegulating hyperscalers

Mains question (10 marks): "Any promise of jobs and prosperity from AI infrastructure must be measured against the land, power and water it consumes." Examine the opportunities and externalities of India's data-centre expansion, and suggest a regulatory framework that balances growth with sustainability and local interests.
