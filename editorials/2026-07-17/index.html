A Grammatical Subject for Democracy: The Politics of Accountability

From exam scandals to polluted air, crumbling cities to unaddressed grievances, public life increasingly runs on the passive voice — where things 'happen' but no one is named as having done them. A hunger strike demanding a single minister's resignation is, at heart, a demand that democratic politics recover a grammatical subject: someone who can be held responsible.

Mains question (10 marks): "Democratic accountability requires a grammatical subject — someone who can be held responsible." Discuss with reference to the convention of ministerial responsibility and the dangers of diffusing blame across 'the system'.

A Transforming NATO and the Space It Opens for India

The 2026 NATO Summit signalled that the alliance is entering a phase of strategic transformation. Its two messages — that Russia remains its principal security challenge for years to come, and that Europe must do far more for its own defence — reshape global supply chains and open new possibilities for a non-aligned but increasingly connected India.

Science, Technology & SecurityRAS Mains · Science & Technology, Cyber Security, Critical Infrastructure

The Cybersecurity Lesson in E-Rickshaws

When some e-rickshaws in Delhi suddenly stalled in the middle of the road, the cause was not a mechanical fault but their Battery Management Systems (BMS) — software-defined, connected systems that could be exploited remotely. The episode is a small warning about a large risk: as everyday devices become connected computers, their security becomes a matter of public safety and critical infrastructure.

In the light of vulnerabilities in EV battery systems, discuss the institutional and policy measures India needs for securing connected devices and critical infrastructure.
