Economy / Trade & External SectorRAS Mains · India's foreign trade; FTAs & economic diplomacy; balance of trade; comparative advantage

Lost in the celebration of trade pacts: why the deal that matters most has no signing ceremony

India has been celebrating a run of trade agreements — the India-UK CETA signed in July 2025 and an India-EU FTA expected to enter force by early 2027. Yet a closer look at the numbers suggests the celebrations may be aimed at the wrong prize. India's single largest goods trade surplus, by a wide margin, is with the United States — a country with which it has no free trade agreement at all. The real strategic question is whether India's trade diplomacy is chasing the deals that improve the margin, or the one that changes the game.

The long road to ethanol: what Brazil's 90-year fuel journey teaches a fast-blending India

As India debates the mileage impact of E20 petrol and pushes toward even higher blends, Brazil's five-decade ethanol success has become a reference point. Brazil's first ethanol blending law dates back to 1931, and its programme was built over decades of graded, milestone-based policy that gave vehicles and consumers time to adapt and offered them real choice. India, by contrast, has moved from about 5% to 20% blending in barely six years — a remarkable feat, but one that raises questions Brazil answered patiently over ninety years.

Accountability after resignation: what tabling the Justice Varma report means for judging judges

The Lok Sabha Speaker's decision to table the report of the parliamentary committee that probed former Allahabad High Court judge Justice Yashwant Varma has pushed India's judicial-accountability framework into uncharted territory. When Justice Varma resigned earlier this year — after wads of burnt currency were reportedly found at his official residence and an in-house Supreme Court panel found him culpable — it was widely assumed the removal process had hit a dead end. Tabling the report challenges a long-held belief: that a judge's resignation automatically extinguishes a parliamentary inquiry into his conduct.
