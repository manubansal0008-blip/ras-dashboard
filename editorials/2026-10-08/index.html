Back to Tightening: Why the RBI Raised Rates After Four Years

Why in news: On 7 October 2026 the RBI's Monetary Policy Committee raised the repo rate by 25 basis points to 5.5% — its first hike in about four years — and raised its FY27 inflation forecast to 5.2%.

Costlier crude (near $120 at times) after West Asia disruptions and rising food prices pushed CPI above the 4% target for three straight months. The RBI chose to act early to anchor inflation expectations and prevent 'second-round effects' on wages and prices, even at some cost to growth.

  • Repo rate: 5.25% → 5.5% (+25 bps)
  • FY27 CPI projection: 5.0% → 5.2%; Q3 peak expected at 5.9%
  • August 2026 CPI: 4.82% (third month above 4%)
  • Inflation target: 4% ± 2% (flexible inflation targeting since 2016)

Way forward: monetary tightening must be paired with supply-side steps — open-market sales of food stocks, calibrated fuel taxes and import facilitation — so that the burden of fighting inflation does not fall on interest rates alone. Clear communication of the policy path (the theme of the 2026 Economics Nobel) is equally important.

Rajasthan angle: higher fuel and food prices hit a large rural and agrarian population; costlier credit affects MSME clusters such as Bhiwadi and textile hubs like Bhilwara.
