Demonetisation at Ten: Cash Is Still King, But Digital Has Arrived

Why in news: Ten years have passed since ₹500 and ₹1,000 notes were withdrawn on 8 November 2016; currency with the public has since risen from ₹17.97 lakh crore to ₹42.55 lakh crore (October 2026).

Demonetisation aimed to curb black money, fake notes and terror funding, and to push digital payments. About 99.3% of the withdrawn value returned to banks, suggesting little black money was held in cash. Yet UPI has grown into the world's largest real-time payment system, and tax filing has widened.

  • Notes withdrawn: 8 Nov 2016 (about 86% of currency by value)
  • About 99.3% of value returned (RBI)
  • Currency with public: ₹17.97 lakh cr → ₹42.55 lakh cr
  • SC upheld the decision 4:1 (Vivek Narayan Sharma case, Jan 2023)

Lesson: big monetary shocks need careful planning and protection for the informal economy; lasting change in payments came from building infrastructure like UPI rather than from the shock itself.

Rajasthan angle: zero-MDR UPI and e-Mitra kiosks have made digital payments common in Rajasthan's towns, but cash still dominates in remote desert villages with weak connectivity.
