Economy & LabourRAS Mains · Indian Economy — employment, labour reforms, social security

From Fewer Jobless to Better Jobs: India's Decent-Work Test

Why in news: Unemployment in India has fallen sharply in official surveys, but an editorial argued that the real test is 'decent work' — jobs with fair pay, security, social protection and dignity.

A low unemployment rate can hide a lot: many Indians cannot afford to be unemployed, so they take any work available — often informal, low-paid and insecure. Counting jobs is therefore not enough; the quality of jobs must be measured.

  • Unemployment rate fell from 6.6% (2017–18) to 3.2% (2023–24) as per annual PLFS
  • ILO's 'decent work' = fair income, rights at work, social protection and dignity; SDG 8 calls for decent work for all
  • A Decent Work Index built on five indicators — opportunity, security, earnings, social security, equality — improved but unevenly
  • Bihar, Jharkhand and Uttar Pradesh lag most; women and informal workers face the largest gaps

Way forward: move from counting jobs to measuring job quality — enforce minimum wages under the Code on Wages, register and cover informal and gig workers, shift workers from farms to productive manufacturing and services, and target lagging states.

Rajasthan angle: the state notified its own Code on Wages rules in 2026 and was the first to legislate for platform gig workers (2023) — both directly shape decent work in the state.

Governance & EconomyRAS Mains · Governance — regulatory reform, ease of doing business

Trust, Clarity, Certainty: What Jan Vishwas Reforms Can Do for Jobs

Why in news: An editorial argued that India needs better wages and more formal jobs, and that the Jan Vishwas approach — trust-based regulation and fewer criminal penalties for minor lapses — can help firms grow and hire formally.

Small firms often stay small and informal because growing brings more inspections, licences and the risk of prosecution for technical lapses. Fear of jail for paperwork errors discourages formal hiring.

  • Jan Vishwas (Amendment of Provisions) Act, 2023 decriminalised minor offences across many central laws
  • Three proposed pillars: Siddhant (trust-based rules), Sangreh (one searchable digital repository of laws), Ashwasan (predictable government services)
  • Decriminalisation replaces jail terms for technical lapses with civil penalties
  • Regulatory complexity raises compliance costs most for MSMEs

Way forward: extend decriminalisation to state laws, publish all rules in one machine-readable portal, set time limits for approvals, and pair regulatory reform with skilling and productivity measures so that formal jobs also pay better.

Rajasthan angle: the state's own Jan Vishwas-style amendments and single-window clearance (Rajasthan Investment Promotion Scheme, One Stop Shop) follow the same logic of trust-based regulation.
