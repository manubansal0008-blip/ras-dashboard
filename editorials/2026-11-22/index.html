One Crore HPV Doses: A Strong Start Towards Ending Cervical Cancer

Why in news: India crossed one crore HPV vaccine doses in under nine months under the national cervical cancer prevention campaign; Rajasthan's coverage stands at 85.23%.

Cervical cancer is largely preventable. Almost all cases are linked to persistent infection with the human papillomavirus, and vaccinating girls before exposure gives lasting protection. India's campaign gives a single free dose to 14-year-old girls with parental consent, and tracks every beneficiary on the U-WIN platform.

  • Doses given: over 1 crore in under 9 months
  • Coverage: ~87% of annual target cohort
  • Top by numbers: UP 29.33 lakh, Bihar 13.70 lakh, Gujarat 7.75 lakh
  • 100% coverage: Gujarat, UP, Andhra Pradesh, Mizoram, MP, Bihar
  • Rajasthan: 85.23%

The speed is impressive, but gaps remain. Coverage varies widely between states, and girls who are out of school or in remote areas are the hardest to reach. Vaccination also protects only future generations; women already exposed need regular screening and early treatment, which remain weak in many districts.

Rajasthan angle: at 85.23%, the State is close to the national average but behind the six states that reached full coverage. Focused drives in districts with low school enrolment of girls, with ASHAs and teachers, can close the gap, while screening at health and wellness centres can protect women already at risk.
