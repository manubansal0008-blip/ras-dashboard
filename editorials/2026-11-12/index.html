Economy & Fiscal FederalismRAS Mains · State finances, taxation, urban land markets

Lower Rates, Higher Revenue: Rethinking Stamp Duty on Property

Why in news: An editorial on Tamil Nadu's stamp duty and registration fee regime argued that high rates and outdated guideline values encourage undervaluation and do not maximise revenue.

Stamp duty and registration fees are among the few large taxes states still control after GST. When the combined rate is high, buyers and sellers under-declare property values, and governments lose both revenue and reliable land-market data.

  • Stamp duty and registration made up about 11.7% of states' own tax revenue in 2024-25
  • Tamil Nadu's combined rate is about 9%, versus about 6% in Karnataka and Maharashtra
  • 2024-25 revenue: Tamil Nadu ~₹22,000 crore, Karnataka ~₹24,000 crore, Maharashtra ~₹50,000 crore
  • Maharashtra caps the registration fee at ₹30,000

Way forward: rationalise rates; cap registration fees instead of charging a percentage; value land using GIS and transaction data; revise guideline values every year through an independent committee; and publish the method.

Rajasthan angle: stamp duty is a major own-tax source for Rajasthan too; the state's DLC rates (district-level committee rates) work like guideline values, and the same reform questions apply.

Environment & Strategic ResourcesRAS Mains · Environment, science & technology — e-waste, critical minerals

Mining the Battery Pile: Recycling as a Critical-Mineral Strategy

Why in news: An editorial argued that recycling end-of-life lithium-ion batteries can supply lithium, nickel and cobalt, cut import dependence and reduce exposure to China's processing dominance.

India has few domestic reserves of battery minerals and depends on imports, mostly processed in China. As electric vehicles multiply, millions of batteries will reach end of life — a 'mine above the ground' if collected and recycled formally.

  • Over 8.7 million EVs were on Indian roads by FY 2025-26
  • Informal recyclers recover only 10–20% of materials; formal plants recover over 90%
  • Recycling could meet 10–12% of India's lithium demand for EV batteries
  • Battery Waste Management Rules, 2022 introduced Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR)

Way forward: buy recycled minerals into a national critical-mineral stockpile, fund R&D for high-purity recovery, set testing standards, make EV makers run take-back schemes, and provide long-term finance for recycling plants.

Rajasthan angle: the Centre's new ₹2,814 crore scheme for lithium and nickel processing and Rajasthan's large solar-plus-battery build-out make battery supply chains a state priority as well.
