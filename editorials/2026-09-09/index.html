Shekhawati's Painted Havelis: From Tentative List to Living Heritage

Why in news: The Painted Haveli Towns of Shekhawati — 14 towns in Jhunjhunu, Sikar and Churu — were added to India's UNESCO Tentative List, now 73 sites strong.

Marwari merchants built these havelis between the 18th and early 20th centuries with earnings from trade. Their frescoes show gods, folk tales, trains and even aeroplanes. Many owners have migrated; havelis lie locked, decaying or sold off in parts.

  • 14 towns incl. Mandawa, Nawalgarh, Fatehpur, Ramgarh, Bisau, Mahansar
  • India's Tentative List: 73 sites (53 cultural, 17 natural, 3 mixed)
  • Rajasthan's World Heritage: Hill Forts, Jantar Mantar, Jaipur city, Keoladeo
  • Tentative listing is the first step to nomination

Way forward: heritage bye-laws for the 14 towns, adaptive reuse as homestays and museums, tax relief for owners who restore, and training of local artisans; then a strong nomination dossier.

Rajasthan angle: the State already has the Jaipur city and Hill Forts inscriptions; a successful Shekhawati bid would spread tourism beyond the Jaipur–Udaipur–Jaisalmer circuit.
