International — ASEAN

PM Modi in the Philippines for the ASEAN-India and East Asia Summits

Rajasthan — Wildlife

Desert foxes to be moved away to protect the Great Indian Bustard

  • The Forest Department plans to make desert foxes shift away from the Sam area of the Desert National Park, Jaisalmer.
  • The step follows the killing of a Great Indian Bustard by a desert fox at a water point on 18 October.
  • Fox dens near bustard habitats will be dismantled so that the foxes move elsewhere.
  • Static GK: The Great Indian Bustard (Godawan) is Rajasthan's State Bird and is listed as Critically Endangered by the IUCN.
  • Static GK: Desert National Park covers parts of Jaisalmer and Barmer districts.

SETU emergency referral network to go statewide

  • It was piloted at RNT Medical College, Udaipur, where it handled more than 3,519 emergency cases from 15 districts.

Polity — Census

Census 2027: self-enumeration portal goes live; process, nodal body and timeline

  • The self-enumeration portal for the population enumeration phase of Census 2027 went live on 16 November 2026 for four poll-bound States, ahead of the rest of the country.
  • Self-enumeration lets a household fill its own details online and get an ID; the enumerator then verifies these details during the door-to-door visit.
  • On the first day the religion question showed a drop-down of 287 entries under 'Other religions and persuasions'; it was withdrawn the same day, with officials saying it was only predictive text to avoid spelling mistakes.
  • The religion question keeps the seven main categories used in earlier Censuses: Hindu, Muslim, Christian, Sikh, Buddhist, Jain and Other religions and persuasions.
  • Nodal body: the Office of the Registrar General and Census Commissioner of India, under the Union Ministry of Home Affairs, conducts the Census under the Census Act, 1948 and Census Rules, 1990.
  • Process: Census 2027 has two phases: Houselisting and Housing Census first, then Population Enumeration; the reference date is 1 March 2027 (1 October 2026 for snow-bound areas).
  • Key features: it is India's first digital Census, with data collected on mobile apps, and the first since 1931 to count caste; it is the 16th Census overall and the 8th since Independence.
Economy — Shipbuilding

Five greenfield shipbuilding clusters: locations, policy and funding

  • India aims to be among the world's top 10 shipbuilding nations by 2030 and the top five by 2047, the Union Ports, Shipping and Waterways Minister said.
  • Investments of nearly $10 billion are projected in five proposed greenfield coastal shipbuilding clusters.
  • Locations: Dugarajapatnam (Andhra Pradesh), Kuchhadi (Gujarat), Dighi (Maharashtra), Kendrapada (Odisha) and Thoothukudi (Tamil Nadu).
  • Policy: the clusters come under the Shipbuilding Development Scheme, part of the ₹69,725 crore maritime package approved by the Union Cabinet in September 2025.
  • The package has three pillars: Shipbuilding Financial Assistance Scheme (about ₹24,736 crore, till 2036), Maritime Development Fund (₹25,000 crore) and Shipbuilding Development Scheme (about ₹19,989 crore) for clusters and capacity.
  • Benefits: domestic shipbuilding capacity of about 4.5 million gross tonnes a year, large employment, lower freight outgo to foreign shipping lines and stronger maritime security.
  • Static GK: India's share of global shipbuilding is under 1%, while China, South Korea and Japan together build most of the world's ships.
Art & Culture — Museums

Dancing Girl and Nizam's jewels to anchor Yuge Yugeen Bharat museum

  • The Dancing Girl of Mohenjodaro, the Nizam's jewels, the Taxila jewellery and the Gandhara collection will be shown in the first gallery, an 'adornments gallery', of the Yuge Yugeen Bharat National Museum.
  • The museum is being set up in the North and South Block buildings of New Delhi's Central Vista at about ₹3,230 crore, after government offices moved out; the Centre says it will have the largest display area of any museum in the world.
  • Historical importance: the bronze Dancing Girl (about 4,500 years old) was found at Mohenjodaro in 1926 and is an early example of the lost-wax casting technique of the Harappan Civilisation.
  • The Nizam's jewels were bought by the Government of India in 1995 and include the Jacob Diamond; the Taxila jewellery and Gandhara sculptures reflect Indo-Greek and Kushana-era art on the ancient Silk Road.
  • Soft-power angle: the museum was announced at the International Museum Expo in 2023 and is being developed with French museum expertise; with the return of stolen antiquities from abroad, it projects India as a civilisational state.
  • It will help tourism, research and the global telling of India's history from the Indus Valley to modern times.
Polity — CAG

Audit Diwas: CAG turns 166 — powers, Articles and landmark audits

  • The Comptroller and Auditor General of India marked Audit Diwas on 16 November 2026, completing 166 years; the day recalls 16 November 1860, when Sir Edward Drummond took charge as the first Auditor General.
  • CAG K. Sanjay Murthy said the institution must use technology fully while staying grounded in its constitutional mandate, and that its job is also to earn people's trust.
  • Articles: Art. 148 (appointment by the President; removal like a Supreme Court judge), Art. 149 (duties and powers), Art. 150 (form of accounts), Art. 151 (reports go to the President for Parliament and to the Governor for State Legislatures).
  • Law and tenure: the CAG (Duties, Powers and Conditions of Service) Act, 1971 sets a term of six years or up to 65 years of age, whichever is earlier; the CAG is not eligible for further office under the Government.
  • Landmark work: audits of 2G spectrum allocation (2010), coal block allocation (2012) and the Commonwealth Games (2011) led to major policy and legal changes.
  • Present CAG: K. Sanjay Murthy, the 15th CAG, in office since November 2024.
  • Static GK: Parliament's Public Accounts Committee examines CAG reports; the CAG is called the 'friend, philosopher and guide' of the PAC.
Geography — Cyclones

Cyclone Akvan likely to cross the Andhra Pradesh coast on 20 November

  • A weather system over the Bay of Bengal was expected to intensify into a severe cyclonic storm, Akvan, and cross the Andhra Pradesh coast on 20 November 2026.
  • Odisha ordered early evacuation of vulnerable southern areas; IMD forecast heavy to very heavy rain in parts of Odisha from 19 to 21 November.
  • Previous impact: Ganjam district has already faced cyclones Arnab and Murasu in three weeks; at least 11 people died in Odisha during Arnab, 10 of them in landslides in Ganjam and Kandhamal.
  • How a cyclone forms: sea-surface temperature above about 26.5°C, a pre-existing low-pressure area, enough Coriolis force (so not right at the equator), high humidity and low vertical wind shear; warm moist air rises, releases latent heat and the system starts rotating anticlockwise in the Northern Hemisphere.
  • IMD scale: depression, deep depression, cyclonic storm (62–88 km/h), severe cyclonic storm (89–117 km/h), very severe, extremely severe and super cyclonic storm (222 km/h and above).
  • Naming: names for the North Indian Ocean come from a list contributed by 13 member countries of the WMO/ESCAP Panel; Akvan was contributed by Iran, Murasu by India and Arnab by Bangladesh.

Science & Technology — Space

Gaganyaan: how India picked its astronauts, and why the mission matters

  • Gaganyaan is India's first human spaceflight mission; crew selection was done jointly by ISRO and the Indian Air Force through a multi-stage process.
  • The Institute of Aerospace Medicine (IAM), Bengaluru, carried out the psychological, physical and clinical evaluation of candidates shortlisted from IAF test pilots.
  • The four astronaut-designates are Group Captains Prasanth Balakrishnan Nair, Ajit Krishnan, Angad Pratap and Shubhanshu Shukla; Shukla flew to the International Space Station on the Axiom-4 mission in 2025.
  • Why test pilots: they are trained to stay calm under high acceleration and sudden cabin depressurisation; historically most first astronauts were military test pilots.
  • Importance: it builds indigenous capability in crew selection, training and bioastronautics, and is a step towards the Bharatiya Antariksh Station and an Indian on the Moon by 2040.
  • Static GK: the Karman line, 100 km above sea level, is widely taken as the edge of space; Wing Commander Rakesh Sharma was the first Indian in space, on Soyuz T-11 in April 1984.
Science & Technology — Cyber Law

Microsoft 365 outage: what Indian law requires from cloud providers

  • A global Microsoft 365 outage on 12–13 November 2026 disrupted Outlook email and other services; airlines such as IndiGo, Akasa Air and Air India reported disruption to email support.
  • Microsoft linked it to a fault in an upgrade of its network capacity and apologised.
  • CERT-In directions (28 April 2022, under the IT Act, 2000) require cyber incidents to be reported within six hours, but they cover attacks and intrusions, not purely technical outages; CERT-In can still seek information under Section 70B(6).
  • RBI's directions on outsourcing risk (28 November 2025) make banks assess dependence on cloud suppliers and keep an exit plan; the RBI holds the bank responsible if the service fails.
  • The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 treats accidental 'loss of access to personal data' as a breach; under its Rules, breaches must be reported to the Data Protection Board and affected persons from May 2027.
  • Lesson: critical sectors need multi-vendor resilience and clearer outage-reporting rules, not just a switch from a global to a domestic vendor.
Economy — Taxation

Capital gains tax and STT: basics, legal provisions and the investor-confidence debate

  • Capital gain is the profit made on selling a capital asset such as shares; it is short-term (STCG) or long-term (LTCG) depending on how long the asset was held — for listed shares, more than 12 months makes it long-term.
  • Law: Section 111A of the Income-tax Act taxes STCG on listed equity, and Section 112A taxes LTCG on listed equity; since July 2024 the rates are 20% for STCG (up from 15%) and 12.5% for LTCG (up from 10%), with LTCG up to ₹1.25 lakh a year exempt.
  • Example (LTCG): buy shares for ₹1,00,000 and sell after two years for ₹3,00,000; gain ₹2,00,000 minus ₹1,25,000 exempt = ₹75,000 taxed at 12.5% = ₹9,375.
  • Example (STCG): buy for ₹1,00,000 and sell after six months for ₹1,50,000; gain ₹50,000 taxed at 20% = ₹10,000.
  • Securities Transaction Tax (STT), introduced by the Finance Act, 2004, is charged on the value of each trade on a stock exchange, whether the trader makes a profit or a loss; the 2026 Union Budget raised STT on futures and options.
  • The debate: one view is that taxing both the transaction (STT) and the gain (LTCG/STCG) adds up to a heavy burden and can hurt market participation and liquidity; the other is that rising markets and fiscal needs justify the higher rates.
  • History: LTCG on listed shares was exempt from 2004 when STT came in, and was brought back in the 2018 Budget at 10% above ₹1 lakh.

Kaziranga road accident: Assam orders probe; road safety plan through the park area

  • Kaziranga National Park lies mainly in Golaghat and Nagaon districts on the floodplains of the Brahmaputra; it became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1985 and a Tiger Reserve in 2006.

Science & Technology — Space

Computing in orbit: AI on board satellites

  • Satellites are beginning to process data in orbit with on-board AI, sending down only useful results instead of all raw images.
  • The European Space Agency's Phi-Sat-1 showed AI selecting Earth-observation images on board, for example by discarding cloud-covered pictures.
  • Benefits: less data to transmit and lower latency, useful for disaster response and defence; future satellites may share data and computing tasks over optical inter-satellite links.
  • Challenges: radiation damage to electronics, limited solar power, heat management, high launch cost and difficulty of in-orbit repair, so satellites need redundancy and error correction.
  • India: the Indian Space Policy 2023 and IN-SPACe (set up in 2020, Ahmedabad) open space to private firms; Hyderabad start-up TakeMe2Space's MOI-1A (October 2026) is India's first AI-based orbital computing satellite.

Rajasthan — Heritage

Bundi's centuries-old stepwells in neglect

  • Many old stepwells (baoris) of Bundi are falling into neglect, and with them traditional water-harvesting knowledge is fading.
  • Bundi is known as the 'City of Stepwells'.
  • Its best-known stepwell, Raniji ki Baori, was built in 1699 by Rani Nathavati, queen of Raja Anirudh Singh, during the reign of her son Rao Raja Budh Singh.
  • Raniji ki Baori is about 46 m deep and known for its carved pillars and gateways.
  • Why it matters: stepwells stored rain and groundwater in a semi-arid region and also served as community spaces.
Rajasthan — Tribal Culture

Notra: the tribal 'crowdfunding' tradition of Vagad

  • Notra is a traditional mutual-aid system among tribal communities of southern Rajasthan, especially the Bhils of the Vagad region.
  • It is practised in Banswara, Dungarpur, Udaipur and Pratapgarh, and in bordering parts of Gujarat and Madhya Pradesh.
  • A family in need invites the village; each guest contributes cash or goods as per capacity, and a record is kept so the help is returned at future Notras.
  • It is used for weddings, illness and house construction, without interest or paperwork, based on trust.
  • Static GK: Vagad mainly refers to the Banswara–Dungarpur region of southern Rajasthan.
