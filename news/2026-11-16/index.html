RAS News Digest — 16 November 2026

Newspaper-based news digest for the RPSC RAS exam, dated 16 November 2026.

Rajasthan — Culture

Jaipur@300: year-long celebrations for the city's 300th foundation year

  • Jaipur will complete 300 years on 18 November 2027, as the city was founded on 18 November 1727.
  • Jaipur Municipal Corporation has begun preparations, with heritage and cultural events every month of the foundation year and global branding of the city.
  • Jaipur natives living abroad will also be invited to the celebrations.
  • Development works worth about ₹1,000 crore, such as elevated roads, underpasses and a master drainage system, are proposed before the foundation year.
  • An 'Ayodhya Parv' showcasing the religious and cultural traditions of Ayodhya is also planned on the lines of Delhi.
  • Static GK: Jaipur was founded by the Kachhwaha ruler Sawai Jai Singh II, and its chief architect was Vidyadhar Bhattacharya.
  • Static GK: The Walled City of Jaipur became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2019; Jantar Mantar (2010) and Amer Fort under the 'Hill Forts of Rajasthan' (2013) are also inscribed.
Important Days

National Press Day — 16 November

  • National Press Day is observed every year on 16 November.
  • The Press Council of India (PCI) began functioning on this day in 1966.
  • The PCI was set up on the recommendations of the First Press Commission, which reported in 1954.
  • The PCI is a statutory, quasi-judicial body that oversees ethical standards in the print media.
  • Static GK: Freedom of the press flows from Article 19(1)(a), and reasonable restrictions are listed in Article 19(2).
  • Static GK: The present PCI works under the Press Council Act, 1978; World Press Freedom Day is on 3 May, and Indian Newspaper Day is on 29 January.
International

UN committee backs moratorium on the death penalty; India votes against

  • On 13 November 2026, the Third Committee of the UN General Assembly (social, humanitarian and cultural) passed a resolution on a moratorium on the use of the death penalty.
  • Mexico moved the resolution, which passed with 133 votes in favour, 33 against and 16 abstentions, and it will now go to the plenary of the General Assembly.
  • India voted against it along with the US, China, Japan, Pakistan, Iran and Saudi Arabia.
  • India said every country has the sovereign right to decide its own legal system, and that India awards the death penalty only in the 'rarest of rare' cases.
  • Of 199 countries, 113 have fully abolished the death penalty, 9 have abolished it for ordinary crimes, 23 have the law but no execution for over 10 years, and 54 still retain it.
  • Static GK: The 'rarest of rare' doctrine comes from Bachan Singh vs State of Punjab (1980).
  • Static GK: The President can pardon a death sentence under Article 72, while the Governor's pardoning power is under Article 161.
Geography — Weather

Cyclone 'Akvan' likely in the Bay of Bengal; western disturbance brings rain to north India

  • The IMD said the depression over the south-east Bay of Bengal may become a cyclonic storm on 16 November and a severe cyclonic storm on 17 November.
  • If it forms, the cyclone will be named 'Akvan', and it may move towards the Andhra Pradesh coast, with heavy to very heavy rain likely in coastal Andhra on 19–20 November.
  • A western disturbance brought rain to Jammu and Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh and Uttarakhand, with snowfall in higher areas.
  • Parts of Delhi, Haryana, Punjab and Rajasthan received light to moderate rain, and a cyclonic circulation has formed over Rajasthan.
  • Static GK: The name 'Akvan' was suggested by Iran, and in the list it comes after 'Arnab' (Bangladesh) and 'Murasu' (India).
  • Static GK: North Indian Ocean cyclones are named from the list of the WMO/ESCAP Panel on Tropical Cyclones (13 members), and RSMC New Delhi (IMD) assigns the names.
  • Static GK: Western disturbances originate over the Mediterranean region, and the winter rain they bring to Rajasthan is called 'Mawath'.
Economy — Trade

Push to cut dependence on China: 90–100% import dependence in 59 product areas

  • India depends on China for 90–100% of its imports in 59 product areas.
  • Examples include leather sofa covers (100%), the antibiotic streptomycin (99.8%), escalators and machinery (98.8%) and vacuum flasks (98.2%).
  • On 19 October, China announced export controls on tunnel boring machines (TBMs) from 18 November 2026, and exporters will need a dual-use export licence.
  • In four years, TBM imports from China fell from about $18 million to $0.8 million, which has affected tunnel projects.
  • India's steps include lithium blocks in Argentina, PLI schemes, anti-dumping duty on Chinese solar panels, three semiconductor plants and upgrades of the Durgapur and Raebareli rail-wheel plants.
  • Static GK: Production Linked Incentive (PLI) schemes began in 2020 and now cover 14 sectors.
  • Static GK: KABIL (Khanij Bidesh India Ltd) acquires critical mineral assets such as lithium and cobalt abroad.
Environment — Audit

CAG audit of Green India Mission: no change seen at 70% of sites

  • A CAG performance audit (Report No. 4 of 2026) covering 16 States and UTs found serious lapses in the Green India Mission (GIM).
  • Satellite images showed no change at about 70% of sites, and saplings were planted at wrong places and in wrong ways.
  • By March 2025, forest cover expansion reached 34,090 hectares against a target of 14 lakh hectares (2.43%), and forest quality improvement reached 1.13 lakh hectares against 14 lakh hectares.
  • Funds from other schemes reached ₹1,149.14 crore against the planned ₹10,600 crore.
  • Parliament's Public Accounts Committee (PAC) has summoned Environment Ministry officials to its meeting on 19 November.
  • Static GK: GIM is one of the eight missions of the National Action Plan on Climate Change (NAPCC, 2008), approved in 2014 under the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change.
  • Static GK: The CAG is covered by Articles 148–151, and the PAC has 22 members (15 Lok Sabha and 7 Rajya Sabha), chaired by convention by the Opposition.
Science & Technology — Digital

Europe moves away from Microsoft; Indian government email shifts to Zoho

  • A Microsoft outage during the EU leaders' summit in Dublin shut down services such as email and intensified the debate on 'digital sovereignty'.
  • French government agencies are moving from Windows to Linux, and the German state of Schleswig-Holstein has begun removing Microsoft Office from about 30,000 computers.
  • Austria's army has replaced Microsoft Office with open-source software on about 16,000 computers, and China is bringing local software into government offices.
  • In India, about 16.68 lakh government email accounts run through NIC have moved to the cloud platform of the Indian company Zoho at a cost of ₹180.10 crore, the IT Ministry told Parliament.
  • Static GK: NIC (National Informatics Centre) works under MeitY and runs government IT and email services.
  • Static GK: BOSS Linux is India's own open-source operating system, developed by C-DAC.
Science & Technology — Space

SpaceX catches a Starship upper stage on the tower for the first time

  • On Flight 15, the upper stage 'Ship 42' returned from space and was caught by the mechanical arms of the launch tower at Starbase, Texas.
  • The catch took place at about 2:51 am IST on 15 November, about 22.5 hours after launch, and it was the first recovery of a Starship upper stage.
  • The ship completed about 14 orbits and deployed 26 Starlink V3 satellites.
  • The plan was to catch both stages, but the Super Heavy booster's return was called off and it fell into the Gulf of Mexico.
  • Static GK: The tower arms are called 'chopsticks', and the whole catching system is called 'Mechazilla'; the Super Heavy booster was first caught on Flight 5 in October 2024.
  • Static GK: India is testing reusable launch technology through ISRO's RLV Landing Experiments (RLV-LEX) at Chitradurga, Karnataka.
Environment — Climate

COP31 in Antalya; India ranks 28th in the Climate Change Performance Index 2027

  • The 31st UN climate conference, COP31, is being held in Antalya, Türkiye, and the heads of government of major countries such as the US, China, India and Russia did not attend.
  • The CCPI 2027 was released on 11 November by Germanwatch, NewClimate Institute and Climate Action Network.
  • India slipped five places from 23rd to 28th with a score of 60.85 in the 'medium' category; as before, the top three places were left empty, and Denmark ranked fourth.
  • The main reason for India's fall was its continued reliance on coal despite rapid growth in renewable energy.
  • India's non-fossil capacity stands at 303.46 GW, about 55% of total installed capacity, against a target of 500 GW by 2030.
  • Static GK: The CCPI rates countries on 14 indicators in four categories: greenhouse gas emissions (40%), renewable energy, energy use and climate policy (20% each).
  • Static GK: India's NDC targets 50% of installed power capacity from non-fossil sources and a 45% cut in emissions intensity (over 2005) by 2030, with net zero by 2070; COP30 was held in Belém, Brazil, in 2025.
Rajasthan — Disaster Management

₹9,321 crore relief package for drought-hit areas of Rajasthan

  • The Rajasthan Government has announced a special relief package of ₹9,321 crore for drought-affected areas.
  • Chief Minister Bhajan Lal Sharma reviewed the situation in Jaipur and ordered arrangements for drinking water, irrigation, fodder and employment.
  • Because of deficient monsoon rain, 87 tehsils in 15 districts have been declared drought-affected.
  • Of these, 73 tehsils face severe drought and 14 face moderate drought.
  • Officials have been told to survey Kharif crop losses and give farmers timely help, with water tankers and fodder depots where needed.
  • Static GK: Drought is declared by the State using the Manual for Drought Management, and relief is paid from the State Disaster Response Fund (SDRF), with extra help from the National Disaster Response Fund (NDRF) in severe cases.
Rajasthan — Industry and Trade

Vanijya Bhawan and Udyog Bhawan open in Jaipur

  • Union Commerce and Industry Minister Piyush Goyal virtually inaugurated Vanijya Bhawan and Udyog Bhawan in Jaipur on 4 November 2026.
  • Jaipur is the first city in the country to bring all departments and attached offices of the Commerce and Industry Ministry under one roof.
  • The buildings house 46 organisations with about 500 offices.
  • Licence, permit and SCOMET approvals will be digitised by 31 March 2027, and from 1 April 2027 these services will need no physical visit.
  • The aim is better coordination, ease of doing business, and more exports and investment.
  • Static GK: SCOMET stands for Special Chemicals, Organisms, Materials, Equipment and Technologies, India's list of dual-use items whose export needs a licence from the DGFT.
