RAS News Digest — 14 July 2026

Newspaper-based news digest for the RPSC RAS exam, dated 14 July 2026.

International Relations

India and New Zealand elevate ties to a Strategic Partnership; adopt 'Roadmap to 2030'

  • During the first visit by an Indian Prime Minister to New Zealand in four decades, both sides raised the relationship to a Strategic Partnership and adopted the 'Roadmap to 2030'.
  • The Roadmap sets an aspirational target of doubling two-way trade to NZ$7 billion (about Rs 35,000 crore) by 2030; goods-and-services trade stood at about USD 2.4 billion in 2024.
  • It covers defence and maritime security (a new annual Maritime Security Dialogue, mutual logistics support), counter-terrorism, agriculture, clean energy and disaster management; New Zealand joined the Global Biofuels Alliance.
  • The Roadmap is non-binding — it creates no legally enforceable obligation or financial commitment, and works only as an implementation framework.

Source: PIB / Ministry of External Affairs, July 2026

Governance & Education

Viksit Bharat Shiksha Adhishthan Bill 2025 under JPC scrutiny as States flag centralisation

  • The Bill proposes a single apex higher-education regulator — the Viksit Bharat Shiksha Adhishthan — replacing the UGC, AICTE and NCTE; medicine and law stay outside its ambit.
  • It will work through three bodies: a Regulatory Council, an Accreditation Council and a Standards Council, with a Chairperson and up to 12 members.
  • Institutions running without approval may face a minimum penalty of Rs 2 crore and closure; appeals lie with the Central Government.
  • States, IITs, IIMs and IISERs have objected on grounds of over-centralisation, loss of institutional autonomy and weak accreditation capacity; the Bill is with a Joint Parliamentary Committee.

Source: Joint Parliamentary Committee / Ministry of Education, July 2026

Economy & Resources

China bans helium exports; India, 100% import-dependent, faces a supply squeeze

  • China imposed an immediate, temporary ban on helium exports to conserve stockpiles for its own AI-chip and quantum-computing industries; it produces only about 1.6% of world helium and imports over 80% of its needs.
  • Global output is concentrated in the United States, Qatar and Russia. India is 100% import-dependent, with more than half its helium coming from Qatar — shipped through the Strait of Hormuz.
  • A March 2026 disruption at Qatar's Ras Laffan complex had already pushed helium prices in India up by 35–50%.
  • Helium is critical for MRI scanners, semiconductor fabrication, ISRO's cryogenic engines, optical fibre and the National Quantum Mission; the Bakreshwar hot springs (West Bengal), with about 1.4% helium, are India's most promising domestic source.

Source: The Hindu / Department of Atomic Energy (VECC), July 2026

Science & Technology

India ranks 9th globally in preparedness to withstand an internet blackout

  • In a study of nearly 100 countries by TRG Datacenters, India ranked 9th with a vulnerability score of 23.4 — a lower score means better preparedness. The United States topped with a score of zero.
  • India is linked to the global internet by only 22 submarine cables — the fewest among the top 10 — against 115 for the US, 72 for Indonesia and 65 for the UK.
  • India's strengths: 40 Internet Exchange Points, about 99.5% electricity access and a cybersecurity readiness score of 98.5 out of 100; about 70% of the population uses the internet.
  • In September 2025, damage to submarine cables near Saudi Arabia slowed internet services across India, Pakistan and the UAE — showing how disruptions far outside Indian waters can hit India.

Source: TRG Datacenters study, July 2026

Polity & Governance

Ladakh to extend Autonomous Hill Councils to all seven districts; 17 new tehsils created

  • The Ladakh administration will extend the Ladakh Autonomous Hill Development Council (LAHDC) framework to all seven districts and has created 17 new tehsils, taking the total to 32.
  • Ladakh earlier had only Leh and Kargil; five new districts — Sham, Nubra, Changthang, Zanskar and Drass — were carved out in April 2026.
  • Each council has directly elected members (single-member constituencies) plus nominated members, a five-year term, and powers over land allotment, local taxes, health, education and tourism.
  • To meet demands for tribal and demographic safeguards, the Centre is weighing a customised model under Article 371 rather than bringing Ladakh under the Sixth Schedule.

Source: Ministry of Home Affairs / Ladakh Administration, July 2026

International Relations

India launches 'SHANTI' vision for its 2028–29 UN Security Council bid

  • India launched its campaign for a non-permanent UN Security Council seat for 2028–29 under the SHANTI vision — Securing Holistic Advancement through Norms, Trust and Integrity.
  • Its planks: a stronger voice for the Global South, reformed and realistically mandated UN peacekeeping, the Women-Peace-Security agenda, and coordinated action on terrorism, climate and maritime security.
  • The UNSC has 5 permanent and 10 non-permanent members; non-permanent members serve two-year terms and need a two-thirds majority in the General Assembly.
  • India has already served eight terms on the Council — most recently in 2021–22 — and has contributed nearly 3 lakh personnel to about 50 UN peacekeeping missions.

Source: Ministry of External Affairs, July 2026

Health & Environment

Parrot Bornavirus 4 detected in India for the first time

  • India has recorded its first confirmed detection and genetic characterisation of Parrot Bornavirus 4 (PaBV-4), a threat to captive and exotic birds.
  • Parrot bornaviruses belong to the genus Orthobornavirus (family Bornaviridae) and infect psittacine birds such as parrots, macaws, cockatoos and budgerigars.
  • PaBV-4 causes Proventricular Dilatation Disease — a neurological and digestive disorder marked by weight loss, regurgitation, poor coordination and death.
  • The virus was found in 44 of 83 birds tested, including 19% of apparently healthy birds, pointing to silent transmission; sequences resemble strains from the US, Canada, Israel, Japan and South Korea, suggesting spread via the international bird trade.

Source: The Hindu / veterinary genomic study, July 2026
