RAS News Digest — 26 November 2026

Newspaper-based news digest for the RPSC RAS exam, dated 26 November 2026.

International Trade

US proposes 25% Section 301 tariff on India, falling to 17% after a trade deal

  • The USTR has proposed a final tariff of 25% on Indian goods after concluding two Section 301 investigations, on goods made with 'forced labour' and on 'industrial capacity'.
  • The rate would drop to 17% once a formal India–US trade deal is signed.
  • GTRI says 17% would give India a 1-point edge over Bangladesh, Cambodia, Indonesia, Malaysia and Thailand (18% deal rate) and a 3-point edge over Vietnam and China (20%).
  • The investigations were launched in March 2026 against 60 economies; in June the USTR had first proposed a 12.5% duty on countries without forced-labour import bans.
  • India's Commerce Ministry said the concerns should be addressed within the ongoing bilateral trade negotiations.
  • Static: Section 301 of the US Trade Act of 1974 lets the USTR act against foreign practices it finds 'unreasonable or discriminatory' that burden US commerce.
Environment

COP31 Antalya: India rejects a uniform timetable for fossil-fuel phase-out

  • Environment Minister Bhupender Yadav said energy transitions must be 'just, orderly, equitable and nationally-determined'.
  • India opposes any single global pathway for moving away from fossil fuels, as it ignores the needs of different countries.
  • COP31 was held at Antalya, Türkiye, with Australia as President of negotiations under a power-sharing deal.
  • The host's main political declaration, the 'Antalya Pact', carried no fossil-fuel roadmap; the EU and many countries rejected it, so for the first time in years the main declaration was not adopted.
  • Background: COP30 at Belém (2025) also ended without a fossil-fuel transition roadmap; COP28 Dubai (2023) first called for 'transitioning away from fossil fuels'.
Economy — Cabinet Decisions

Cabinet clears ₹11,000-crore equipment scheme, Silchar airport, Kamarajar terminal and urea plants

  • Construction & Infrastructure Equipment (CIE): an ₹11,000-crore scheme to build a domestic manufacturing ecosystem for CIE and cut import dependence.
  • Greenfield airport at Doloo, Silchar (Cachar, Assam) at ₹2,014 crore; Assam is handing about 992 acres of Doloo Tea Estate land to AAI, as the existing Kumbhirgram airport is a defence airfield that cannot be expanded.
  • Second container terminal at Kamarajar Port (Ennore, Tamil Nadu) to be built under PPP.
  • MoU with Singapore on aircraft Maintenance, Repair and Overhaul (MRO).
  • Continuation of the Postal Operations (₹1,141 crore) and Estates Management (₹1,491 crore) schemes for the 16th Finance Commission cycle.
  • Six new urea plants approved; the National Investment Policy for Urea-2026 (NIPU-2026, July 2026) aims at 8–9 new gas-based plants of about 12.7 lakh tonnes each.
International Relations

NSA Doval at Colombo Security Conclave in Malé: India pledges 'all necessary help'

  • NSA Ajit Doval said the Indian Ocean region faces 'serious security challenges' and India will work with CSC members to build collective capacities.
  • He called the CSC the meeting point of India's Neighbourhood First policy and the MAHASAGAR vision.
  • Members: India, Maldives, Sri Lanka, Mauritius, Bangladesh and Seychelles; Seychelles' accession as a full member was welcomed at the 7th NSA-level meeting in New Delhi (20 Nov 2025).
  • Five pillars: maritime safety and security; countering terrorism and radicalisation; combating trafficking and transnational organised crime; cyber security and critical infrastructure; humanitarian assistance and disaster relief.
  • Origin: began in 2011 as a trilateral (India–Sri Lanka–Maldives) maritime security grouping; renamed CSC in 2020, and Mauritius joined as the fourth member at the 5th NSA meeting in Malé (2022).
Polity

Constitution Day (Samvidhan Divas) — 26 November

  • The Constituent Assembly adopted the Constitution on 26 November 1949; it came fully into force on 26 January 1950.
  • 26 November has been observed as Constitution Day since 2015, the 125th birth anniversary year of Dr B.R. Ambedkar, chairman of the Drafting Committee.
  • On 25 November the Youth Affairs Ministry held MY Bharat Yuva Samvad events ahead of the day.
  • Static: some provisions (citizenship, elections, provisional Parliament) came into force on 26 November 1949 itself under Article 394.
Polity — Anti-defection

SC hears Shiv Sena (UBT) plea: legislative majority cannot become the 'original party'

  • The Supreme Court is hearing the Uddhav Thackeray faction's challenge (lead plea by Sunil Prabhu) to the Maharashtra Speaker's 10 January 2024 decision not to disqualify Shinde-faction MLAs.
  • Counsel Kapil Sibal argued that under the Tenth Schedule a legislative majority cannot turn a legislature party into the original political party.
  • He warned that if shifting allegiance 'under the guise of dissent' is allowed, elected governments can be toppled by engineering defections; the Bench is likely to reserve its verdict.
  • Static: the Tenth Schedule was added by the 52nd Amendment (1985); the 91st Amendment (2003) deleted the 'split' exception and kept only merger by two-thirds of the legislature party.
  • Kihoto Hollohan (1992) upheld the Schedule and made the Speaker's decision subject to judicial review.
Education — Language Policy

Three-language formula: SC says no mandatory switch midway through this academic year

  • The Supreme Court said Class 6 students who chose two languages this academic year need not be forced to switch to three midway.
  • The order came on a CBSE plea to modify the court's 28 September order, which had extended to Class 6 the transitional relaxation given to Classes 7, 8 and 9.
  • Under the relaxation, the third language is assessed only through internal school-based evaluation, with no pass/fail link to Board results in Class 10.
  • The court had earlier proposed making the scheme mandatory for Class 6 only from 1 January 2027, to avoid 'springing a surprise' on the current batch.
  • Static: the three-language formula dates to the Kothari Commission (1964-66) and the 1968 education policy; NEP 2020 keeps it with flexibility and no language imposed on any state.
Polity — Languages

West Bengal CM says Rajbanshi will be included in the Eighth Schedule

  • West Bengal CM Suvendu Adhikari said at Cooch Behar that Rajbanshi will be included in the Eighth Schedule of the Constitution.
  • Rajbanshi (closely linked to Kamtapuri) is spoken in north Bengal, lower Assam and parts of Bihar, Nepal and Bangladesh.
  • Static: the Eighth Schedule now lists 22 languages; 14 were in the original Constitution. Sindhi was added in 1967 (21st Amendment), Konkani, Manipuri and Nepali in 1992 (71st), and Bodo, Dogri, Maithili and Santali in 2003 (92nd).
  • Inclusion needs a constitutional amendment by Parliament; there are no fixed criteria, and the Pahwa (1996) and Sitakant Mohapatra (2003) committees failed to settle them.
  • Rajasthan link: inclusion of Rajasthani in the Eighth Schedule is a long-pending demand; the Rajasthan Assembly passed a resolution for it in 2003.
Economy

Rupee turns from Asia's worst to best performer, nears 91 per dollar

  • The rupee touched an eight-month high of 92.18 per dollar intraday and closed at 92.24 on Wednesday, up 0.6%.
  • It has risen almost 3% in November after nearly touching 97 per dollar in May.
  • Drivers: the RBI's clampdown on speculative bets against the rupee and record FPI inflows into Indian stocks.
  • Static: India follows a managed float; the RBI intervenes through spot and forward markets using forex reserves.
Agriculture & IPR

Hapus GI dispute: Konkan growers oppose 'Valsad Hapus' tag

  • Alphonso (Hapus) mangoes from Ratnagiri, Sindhudurg, Raigad, Thane and Palghar got a GI tag in October 2018.
  • An application for 'Valsad Hapus' (Gujarat) was filed on 13 February 2023 by Bharatiya Kisan Sangh, Gandhinagar and Navsari Agricultural University.
  • Konkan GI holders rejected a settlement suggested by the GI Registry; the final hearing is over and the decision is reserved.
  • Static: GIs are registered under the Geographical Indications of Goods (Registration and Protection) Act, 1999 by the GI Registry, Chennai; protection lasts 10 years and is renewable.

India Pavilion wins the Visitors' Lion at the 61st Venice Art Biennale

  • The Pavilion of India won the Visitors' Lion at the 61st International Art Exhibition – La Biennale di Venezia 2026 (PIB, 25 Nov).

PIB in Brief

Antivenom for pit vipers, 10 crore MSME registrations, new services price index

  • Health: India's first North-East-specific antivenom against pit vipers has been developed; current polyvalent antivenom targets the 'Big Four' (cobra, common krait, Russell's viper, saw-scaled viper) and works poorly on NE pit vipers.
  • MSME: registrations on the Udyam and Udyam Assist platforms have crossed 10 crore.
  • MoSPI: released provisional Services Producer Price Indices (SPPI) with base 2022-23, along with the report of the Sub-Committee on Regional Accounts and a flash report on 1,862 central infrastructure projects.
  • Defence: India–Nepal joint military exercise Surya Kiran (20th edition) began in Nepal.
Economy — External Sector

Net FDI turns negative at –$4.95 billion in September, lowest since 2021

  • RBI data show net FDI in September 2026 was –$4.95 billion, the lowest since at least March 2021, as gross inflows slumped and outflows grew about 40%.
  • For April–September 2026, net FDI was still more than 20% higher than a year earlier.
  • Net FDI = gross inflows minus repatriation/disinvestment by foreign investors and outward FDI by Indian firms.
  • FDI gives the investor lasting control (10% or more equity); FPI is a smaller, more liquid portfolio stake.
  • Same day: IRDAI set up two study groups under its Insurance Advisory Committee to standardise health-insurance claim metrics, reason codes and real-time claim tracking.
Health

287 drug samples flagged 'not of standard quality' by State labs in October

  • State Drug Testing Laboratories flagged 287 samples as Not of Standard Quality (NSQ) in October; Central Drugs Laboratories flagged 37.
  • The list of NSQ and spurious drugs is put on the CDSCO portal every month.
  • Static: drug quality is regulated under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940; CDSCO under the DGCI is the national regulator; Revised Schedule M (GMP) became mandatory for MSME makers from 1 January 2026.
