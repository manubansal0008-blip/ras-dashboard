RAS News Digest — 12 July 2026

Newspaper-based news digest for the RPSC RAS exam, dated 12 July 2026.

International Relations

India and New Zealand elevate ties to a strategic partnership

  • India and New Zealand have elevated their relationship to a strategic partnership, deepening cooperation across trade, defence, education, agriculture and people-to-people links.
  • The two sides are negotiating a Free Trade Agreement (talks launched in 2025); dairy market access and services are among the key issues.
  • New Zealand is a Pacific country (capital Wellington) and a Commonwealth member; it is not part of the Quad, which comprises India, the United States, Japan and Australia.

Source: Ministry of External Affairs, July 2026

Defence & Security

INS Mahendragiri, a Project 17A stealth frigate, joins the Navy

  • The Indian Navy commissioned INS Mahendragiri, a stealth frigate built under Project 17A (the Nilgiri class), reflecting India's push for indigenous, self-reliant warship-building.
  • It is named after the Mahendragiri peak in the Eastern Ghats; Project 17A frigates carry advanced sensors, weapons and stealth features for surface, air and anti-submarine warfare.
  • The commissioning came alongside the Navy highlighting its role in escorting Indian merchant vessels through the West Asia conflict zone, underscoring maritime security for trade and energy.

Source: Indian Navy / Ministry of Defence, July 2026

Health / Economy

NPPA fixes the retail prices of 39 new drug formulations

  • The National Pharmaceutical Pricing Authority (NPPA) fixed the retail prices of 39 new drug formulations to keep essential medicines affordable.
  • The NPPA regulates the prices of scheduled drugs under the Drugs (Prices Control) Order (DPCO), 2013, and monitors the prices of non-scheduled drugs.
  • It functions under the Department of Pharmaceuticals in the Ministry of Chemicals and Fertilizers — not the RBI or the health ministry directly.

Source: NPPA / Department of Pharmaceuticals, July 2026

Infrastructure / Aviation

Airports Authority of India steps up airport development and expansion

  • The Airports Authority of India (AAI) is expanding airport infrastructure — new terminals, greenfield airports and upgrades — to meet fast-growing air traffic and support the UDAN regional connectivity scheme.
  • The AAI is a statutory body under the Ministry of Civil Aviation, formed in 1995 by merging the National Airports Authority and the International Airports Authority of India.
  • It manages a large network of airports and provides air navigation services across Indian airspace.

Source: Airports Authority of India / Ministry of Civil Aviation, July 2026

Defence / Maritime Security

Indian Navy's West Asia mission protected merchant shipping, says Rajnath

  • The Defence Minister said the Indian Navy escorted 18 merchant vessels carrying essential cargo worth thousands of crores through the West Asia conflict zone, protecting India's energy and trade interests.
  • A large share of India's crude oil and LNG imports pass through the region, so safe sea lanes are vital; the Strait of Hormuz — a key oil chokepoint — connects the Persian Gulf to the Gulf of Oman, not the Bay of Bengal.
  • He linked the effort to indigenous shipbuilding and a self-reliant, blue-water Navy able to secure distant sea lanes.

Source: Ministry of Defence, July 2026

Environment / Urban Governance

Monsoon exposes urban flooding as cities struggle with drainage

  • Heavy monsoon rain has again left several Indian cities waterlogged, exposing weak stormwater drainage, unplanned construction and the loss of natural water bodies and floodplains.
  • Urban flooding is worsened by concretisation that reduces ground-water recharge and by encroachment on drains and lakes.
  • Solutions stressed include better drainage planning, rainwater harvesting, protecting wetlands, and sponge-city approaches that let cities absorb and store rainwater.

Source: Urban local bodies / press, July 2026

International Relations

15 Indian tourists killed in a Vietnam boat tragedy

  • Fifteen Indian tourists were killed when a tourist boat capsized in Vietnam, prompting Indian missions to assist the families and coordinate on repatriation.
  • The tragedy underlines the safety of Indians travelling abroad and India's consular role in protecting its citizens overseas.
  • Vietnam is a Southeast Asian country and a member of ASEAN (not SAARC); India-Vietnam ties are a Comprehensive Strategic Partnership.

Source: Ministry of External Affairs, July 2026
