India–Israel investment treaty and the new BIT model

International Relations / EconomyRAS Mains GS-II · India & the world; economic diplomacy

Question (10 marks, ~150 words):

The India–Israel Bilateral Investment Agreement reopens India's investment-treaty regime to portfolio capital. Examine the opportunities and the risks it carries.

Model Answer

Introduction: The India-Israel Bilateral Investment Agreement (BIA) marks a cautious re-opening of India's investment-treaty regime. After scrapping most old BITs, India had adopted a defensive 2015 Model BIT requiring five years' exhaustion of local remedies before international arbitration and excluding portfolio investment. The question demands examining the opportunities and risks of softening this stance.

Opportunities:

  • As India's first BIA with an OECD member, it signals a predictable, investor-friendly climate, cutting the local-remedies exhaustion period from five to three years and reassuring investors after the earlier scrapping.
  • Crucially, it covers shares, stocks, equity, qualifying bonds, loans and corporate debt, deepening capital-market linkages beyond factory FDI, and aims to lift the modest $371.35 million FDI base (April 2000-March 2026).
  • It also sets a template as India negotiates with Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Oman, Switzerland, Russia, Australia and the EU, advancing economic diplomacy alongside a slow-moving India-Israel FTA.

Risks:

  • GTRI's Ajay Srivastava warns that portfolio coverage widens India's exposure to investor-state disputes beyond traditional FDI.
  • Volatile 'hot money' can complicate the policy autonomy the 2015 model deliberately protected.

Way forward:

  • Use the BIA to refine a revamped model treaty that welcomes portfolio capital yet ring-fences legitimate regulatory space.
  • Apply the template thoughtfully to future talks.

Conclusion: Its worth ultimately depends on drawing in capital without surrendering regulatory sovereignty.
