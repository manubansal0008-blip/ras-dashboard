Justice behind bars: legal aid, poor prisoners and the gendered gap

Governance / Social JusticeRAS Mains · Governance; access to justice & legal aid; vulnerable sections (women prisoners)

Question (10 marks, ~150 words):

Despite a robust constitutional and statutory framework, effective legal aid still eludes India's poor and women prisoners. Critically examine the gaps in access to justice for prisoners and suggest gender-sensitive reforms.

Model Answer

Introduction:

  • Access to justice is a cornerstone of the rule of law; Article 39A, Article 21/22(1) and the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987 (NALSA) give India a strong legal-aid framework, yet prisons stay crowded with undertrials.

The gaps:

  • Delivery deficit: judicial delay, congestion and late/no legal aid keep the undertrial share high; a pervasive awareness gap leaves the poorest unable to claim aid.
  • Gender-blindness: women prisoners are an 'invisible population' — poverty, stigma, healthcare and motherhood needs, and male-designed systems.
  • Quality deficit: under-paid, over-loaded panel lawyers give token, not effective, representation.
  • Structural bias: over-representation of poor and lower-caste inmates mirrors wider inequality.

Reform levers:

  • BNSS Section 479 (undertrial relief) and the Model Prisons and Correctional Services Act, 2023 provide a corrective base.

Gender-sensitive reforms:

  • Prison-based paralegals and legal-aid clinics from day one; audit of panel-lawyer pay and quality; women's wards with healthcare, creche and trauma-informed rehabilitation; e-prisons and video hearings against delay; family contact to cut recidivism.

Conclusion:

  • A sentence should cost liberty, not the right to a fair defence; the promise exists — implementation must reach the illiterate undertrial and the stigmatised woman.
