From uranium to the Indo-Pacific: the Melbourne summit and the maturing India-Australia partnership

International RelationsRAS Mains · India's bilateral relations; Indo-Pacific & Quad; energy & critical-mineral security

Question (10 marks, ~150 words):

'The India-Australia partnership is a case of complementary strengths meeting a shared strategic concern.' Discuss the key pillars of this relationship and examine its significance for India's energy security and Indo-Pacific strategy.

Model Answer

Introduction:

  • The PM's state visit to Australia (8-10 July 2026) yielded a broad package across defence, civil nuclear energy, critical minerals and trade, marking a maturing partnership.

Key pillars:

  • Civil nuclear: operationalising the 2014 deal for a stable uranium corridor.
  • Critical minerals: lithium, cobalt, rare earths to de-risk clean-energy/electronics supply chains from China.
  • Defence: interoperability, exercises, defence-industrial links.
  • Economy: fast-tracking an upgrade of the 2022 ECTA; diaspora and education links.

Strategic significance:

  • Both are Quad democracies invested in a free, open, rules-based Indo-Pacific and freedom of navigation; the tie hedges against an assertive China.

Caveats:

  • Trade remains modest; Australia's economic dependence on China means interests converge but are not identical.

Rajasthan relevance:

  • External uranium complements domestic finds (Rohil, Sikar); critical minerals support the state's solar build-out.

Conclusion:

  • Substance over symbolism — the task is delivery: completing the trade deal and converting declarations into supply and joint production.
