Trading blows amid talks: the US, Iran and the fragile future of the Strait of Hormuz

International RelationsRAS Mains · International relations; West Asia; energy security & the Strait of Hormuz (India's stakes)

Question (10 marks, ~150 words):

The Strait of Hormuz has become both a bargaining chip and a flashpoint in US-Iran relations. Discuss its strategic significance and examine the implications of West Asian instability for India's energy security and foreign policy.

Model Answer

Introduction:

  • The US declaring the Iran ceasefire 'over' amid continuing talks, with strikes in Bahrain, Kuwait and Iran, keeps the Strait of Hormuz — the world's key oil chokepoint — on edge.

Strategic significance of Hormuz:

  • A majority of seaborne crude and much of India's oil/LNG transit it; its long-term administration is to be shared by Oman and Iran, but the June 17 MoU yielded no lasting deal.
  • UNCLOS guarantees transit passage (Art 38) and bars navigation charges (Art 26); Art 43 urges cooperation on such straits.

The wider theatre:

  • Iraq (US withdrawal, entrenched PMF), Lebanon (June 26 framework, Hizbollah disarmament stalemate), and Gulf mediation (Qatar, Oman, Saudi) shape a managed rivalry.
  • Energy interdependence (North Field/South Pars, Gulf power links) incentivises avoiding war.

Implications for India:

  • Energy security depends on open Hormuz; India must balance Israel, the Gulf and Iran (Chabahar), protect its diaspora, and diversify sources/routes.

Conclusion:

  • The likeliest path is a combustible but managed rivalry; India's response is quiet, consistent diplomacy — de-escalation, energy diversification and open channels to all sides.
