Viksit Bharat Shiksha Adhishthan Bill: One Regulator, Many Doubts

Polity & GovernanceRAS Mains · Polity & Governance, Education, Centre–State Relations

Question (10 marks, ~150 words):

The Viksit Bharat Shiksha Adhishthan Bill, 2025 seeks to simplify higher-education regulation, but has raised concerns about institutional autonomy and centralisation. Discuss.

Model Answer

Introduction:

  • The VBSA Bill, 2025 proposes a single apex higher-education regulator replacing the UGC, AICTE and NCTE, operating through Regulatory, Accreditation and Standards Councils — the structure envisaged by NEP 2020.

The case for consolidation:

  • Ends the fragmented, overlapping mandates of three regulators and reduces compliance burden on institutions.
  • Separates regulation, accreditation and standard-setting — a long-standing recommendation of the Yashpal Committee and the National Knowledge Commission.
  • Graded penalties and clear degree-granting rules curb fly-by-night institutions.

Concerns raised:

  • Federalism: education is in the Concurrent List, yet States and State Higher Education Councils get thin representation.
  • Autonomy: IITs, IIMs and IISERs argue the Bill overrides their founding Acts, creating legal ambiguity for INIs and IoEs.
  • Adjudication: appeals lie with the Central Government, not an independent tribunal — the regulator becomes judge in its own cause.
  • Capacity: the accreditation system inherited is weak, with low coverage and credibility questions around NAAC.
  • Proportionality: a Rs 2 crore penalty floor can shut small rural and tribal colleges facing genuine resource constraints.

Way forward:

  • Statutory autonomy carve-outs for INIs/IoEs, meaningful State representation, an independent educational tribunal for appeals, and a separate Higher Education Grants Council to insulate funding from regulation.

Conclusion:

  • Simplification must not become centralisation; the Bill's success depends on writing autonomy and federal balance into the statute itself.
