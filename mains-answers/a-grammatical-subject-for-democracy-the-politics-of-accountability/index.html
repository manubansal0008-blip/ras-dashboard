A Grammatical Subject for Democracy: The Politics of Accountability

Polity & GovernanceRAS Mains · Polity & Governance, Accountability, Ethics in Public Life

Question (10 marks, ~150 words):

"Democratic accountability requires a grammatical subject — someone who can be held responsible." Discuss with reference to the convention of ministerial responsibility and the dangers of diffusing blame across 'the system'.

Model Answer

Introduction:

  • A weeks-long hunger strike seeking a minister's resignation over an alleged exam-paper leak raises a deeper question: modern governance increasingly describes failure in the passive voice, where things 'happen' but no one is named responsible.

The problem:

  • Subjectless sentences ('mistakes were made') diffuse blame across 'the system', freeing every individual from the duty to answer or act.
  • This erodes the incentive to decide well and to prevent the next failure.

The constitutional anchor:

  • Ministerial responsibility is a settled convention of parliamentary democracy; owning failure sustains public trust and the rule of law.
  • Peaceful protest and judicial concern for a protester's health show institutions functioning.

The caution:

  • Not every systemic failure is one person's fault; resignation as spectacle must not replace structural reform.

Way forward:

  • Fix responsibility clearly on those who decided, and pair it with reforms that prevent recurrence; protect the right to protest.

Conclusion:

  • Restoring a grammatical subject to public life is the everyday discipline that keeps a self-governing people in charge of its institutions.
