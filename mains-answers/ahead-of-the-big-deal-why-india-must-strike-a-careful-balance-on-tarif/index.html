Ahead of the Big Deal: Why India Must Strike a Careful Balance on Tariffs

Economy & International RelationsRAS Mains · Economy, External Sector, Trade Policy, India-US Relations

Question (10 marks, ~150 words):

"Trade pressure is best treated as leverage, not destiny." In the light of recent US tariff moves, examine how India can balance market access with strategic autonomy in its external economic policy.

Model Answer

Introduction:

  • The US has moved on tariffs — a Section 301 action on about 60 countries and a Bill targeting buyers of Russian oil — with a ~150-day universal-tariff window closing around 24 July, framing a moment of leverage rather than a fixed outcome.

India's stakes:

  • The US is India's largest export market; a balanced deal secures access for goods and services and reduces uncertainty.
  • Diversified crude sourcing (Russia, Gulf, US) keeps energy affordable and is itself a bargaining asset.

The risks:

  • Over-reliance on discounted Russian oil invites secondary-tariff exposure; a deadline can force premature concessions.
  • Disruptive openings in agriculture, dairy or MSMEs would hit livelihoods.

Way forward:

  • Negotiate a calibrated, sector-by-sector deal — measured openings for durable market access and predictable rules.
  • Stay WTO-consistent, keep sourcing diversified, and shield sensitive sectors.

Conclusion:

  • A deal under pressure should be judged by how it balances access with autonomy — firm on core interests, flexible on detail, anchored in the rules.
