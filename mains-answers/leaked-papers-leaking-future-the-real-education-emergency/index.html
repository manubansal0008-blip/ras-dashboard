Leaked Papers, Leaking Future: The Real Education Emergency

Education & EconomyRAS Mains · Human Capital, Public Finance, Governance, Education Policy

Question (10 marks, ~150 words):

"The examination crisis is a symptom; chronic under-investment in education is the disease." Critically examine this statement in the context of India's demographic dividend, and suggest reforms in the financing and governance of school and higher education.

Model Answer

Introduction:

  • The NEET leak and the demand for a retest have triggered a national protest, but the deeper failure is structural: education's share in total Union expenditure has fallen over 12 years even as a young India waits to join the growth story.

Symptom vs disease:

  • Leaks, re-conducted exams and coaching dependence reflect an unaccountable examination architecture (echoing the NEET-UG 2024 crisis and the Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Act, 2024).
  • The disease is under-investment: NEP 2020's 6%-of-GDP goal remains distant, and employability — not selection ratios — is the binding constraint on the demographic dividend.

Financing reforms:

  • Raise and protect education's expenditure share; outcome-linked transfers to states; fund foundational literacy (NIPUN Bharat) and vocational skilling at scale.

Governance reforms:

  • Secure, audited examination systems with fixed accountability; a strengthened, transparent NTA.
  • Concurrent-List design demands genuine Centre-State coordination — curriculum, language policy and admissions cannot be run by fiat.

Way forward:

  • Treat exam security as the entry point to systemic reform — teacher quality, learning outcomes and employability as the true metrics.

Conclusion:

  • A generation that votes for the first time in 2029 will judge the gap between promise and delivery; investing in its education is the real test of the India story.
