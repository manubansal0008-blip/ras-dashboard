Inflation Ticks Up: The RBI's Balancing Act

EconomyRAS Mains · Economy, Monetary Policy, Inflation, Cost of Living

Question (10 marks, ~150 words):

Fighting inflation with interest rates can slow growth, especially when prices are driven by supply shocks. In this light, examine the challenges before India's monetary policy.

Model Answer

Introduction:

  • Retail (CPI) inflation at a 17-month high, above 4%, has revived expectations of an RBI rate move in August — reopening the classic tension between controlling prices and protecting growth.

The framework:

  • The RBI targets CPI inflation at 4% within a +/- 2% band; the Monetary Policy Committee sets the repo rate.
  • Raising rates cools demand but also slows credit, investment and growth.

Why rate hikes alone may not work:

  • If inflation is driven by food and supply shocks (potato, vegetables), demand-side tightening does little about the real cause.
  • Costlier home and auto loans squeeze the middle class and soft rural demand.

Way forward:

  • Attack supply-side inflation with better storage, buffer stocks and smoother logistics.
  • Keep monetary policy data-led; avoid over-tightening a fragile recovery.

Conclusion:

  • Inflation is a tax nobody votes for; managing it means balancing firmness to protect savings against the risk of choking the growth that lifts incomes.
