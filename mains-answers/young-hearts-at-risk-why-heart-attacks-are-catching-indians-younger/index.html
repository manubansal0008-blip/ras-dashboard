Young hearts at risk: why heart attacks are catching Indians younger

Health / SocietyRAS Mains · Public health; non-communicable diseases; lifestyle, stress & pollution; preventive healthcare

Question (10 marks, ~150 words):

'A heart attack in a young Indian is usually a preventable outcome, not fate.' Discuss the factors behind the rising incidence of heart disease among young Indians and suggest a balanced strategy combining individual and public-health measures.

Model Answer

Introduction:

  • Heart attacks are increasingly striking Indians in their 20s-40s — a shift signalling a growing non-communicable disease (NCD) burden.

Why it is rising:

  • Chronic stress (job/financial pressure, social media); diets high in sugar, trans fats and fast food; sedentary, screen-bound lifestyles.
  • Tobacco and alcohol; air pollution (PM2.5) causing inflammation; an Indian genetic predisposition to premature coronary disease and diabetes.

The mechanism:

  • A heart attack is the end-point of years of silent arterial damage — age is no longer a shield.

A balanced strategy:

  • Individual: 30-45 minutes of daily activity, balanced diet, quitting tobacco, stress management, regular screening.
  • Public-health/policy: sugar/tobacco taxes and food labelling, clean air and walkable cities, universal early screening for BP/sugar/cholesterol, workplace and school health.

Rajasthan relevance:

  • Rising urban NCD burden; health-assurance schemes and wellness centres can shift from treating to preventing heart disease.

Conclusion:

  • Pair personal responsibility with an enabling environment; prevention over cure is the most cost-effective investment.
