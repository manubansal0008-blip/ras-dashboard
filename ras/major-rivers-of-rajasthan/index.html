Rivers of Rajasthan

Rajasthan is India's driest large state, and its rivers reflect that: the Chambal is the only perennial river, the Banas is the longest river flowing entirely within Rajasthan, and the Luni ends not in a sea but in the Rann of Kutch. The Aravalli range is the great divide — rivers east of it drain to the Bay of Bengal, those west of it to the Arabian Sea, and a third group simply disappears inland.

Chambal — the only perennial river

The Chambal is Rajasthan's only perennial (year-round) river. It rises from the Janapav hills of the Vindhyan range in Madhya Pradesh, enters Rajasthan at Chaurasigarh, and flows through Kota, Bundi, Sawai Madhopur, Karauli and Dholpur before joining the Yamuna in Uttar Pradesh. It carries Rajasthan's biggest dam cluster and the National Chambal Sanctuary, famous for the gharial and the Gangetic dolphin.

  • Origin: Janapav hills, Vindhyan range (Madhya Pradesh)
  • Only perennial river of Rajasthan; joins the Yamuna
  • Tributaries: Banas, Kali Sindh, Parvati, Kunu, Mej
  • Dams: Gandhi Sagar, Rana Pratap Sagar, Jawahar Sagar and the Kota Barrage
  • National Chambal Sanctuary — gharial, Gangetic dolphin, smooth-coated otter

Banas — the longest river wholly within Rajasthan

The Banas ('van-ki-asha', hope of the forest) rises from the Khamnor hills of the Aravalli in Rajsamand district and is the longest river flowing entirely inside Rajasthan. It runs through Rajsamand, Bhilwara, Ajmer, Tonk and Sawai Madhopur, and merges into the Chambal near Rameshwaram. The Bisalpur dam on the Banas is the drinking-water lifeline of Jaipur, Ajmer and Tonk.

  • Origin: Khamnor hills, Aravalli (Rajsamand district)
  • Longest river flowing entirely within Rajasthan
  • Tributaries: Berach, Kothari, Khari, Dai, Morel, Menali
  • Bisalpur dam (Tonk) — drinking water for Jaipur, Ajmer and Tonk
  • Merges into the Chambal near Rameshwaram (Sawai Madhopur)

Luni, Mahi and the inland rivers

The Luni is the lifeline of western Rajasthan. Rising from the Naga hills near Ajmer, it flows south-west through Pali, Barmer and Jalore and ends in the Rann of Kutch — reaching no sea. Its water is sweet up to Balotra and turns saline after, which is why it is called the 'salt river'. The Mahi enters Banswara from Madhya Pradesh, is dammed at Mahi Bajaj Sagar, and is the only Indian river to cross the Tropic of Cancer twice. The Ghaggar, often identified with the ancient Saraswati, is an inland 'dead river' that dies out in Hanumangarh.

  • Luni: origin Naga hills (Ajmer); ends in the Rann of Kutch; sweet till Balotra, saline after
  • Luni tributaries: Jojari (only left-bank), Sukri, Bandi, Jawai, Khari
  • Mahi: Mahi Bajaj Sagar dam (Banswara); crosses the Tropic of Cancer twice
  • Ghaggar: inland 'dead river', linked to the ancient Saraswati; ends near Hanumangarh
  • Other inland rivers: Kantli (Jhunjhunu), Kakni (Jaisalmer), Sabi and Ruparel

How rivers are asked in the RAS exam

  • The single most repeated fact: the Chambal is Rajasthan's only perennial river, and the Banas is the longest river flowing entirely within the state. Do not confuse the two.
  • Origin points are a favourite trap — Chambal from Janapav (Madhya Pradesh), Banas from Khamnor (Rajsamand), Luni from the Naga hills (Ajmer).
  • Remember the Aravalli as the water divide: it separates Bay-of-Bengal drainage from Arabian-Sea drainage, with a third inland group that reaches no sea at all.
  • Link rivers to their dams (Bisalpur on Banas, Mahi Bajaj Sagar on Mahi, Gandhi Sagar on Chambal) — questions often pair a dam with the wrong river.

Frequently asked questions

Which is the only perennial river of Rajasthan?

The Chambal. It rises from the Janapav hills of the Vindhyan range in Madhya Pradesh and is the only river in Rajasthan that flows all year round.

Which is the longest river flowing entirely within Rajasthan?

The Banas, which rises from the Khamnor hills of the Aravalli in Rajsamand and joins the Chambal near Rameshwaram.

Why is the Luni called a salt river?

Its water stays sweet up to Balotra and turns saline downstream because of the salt-rich soils it passes through. It ends in the Rann of Kutch without reaching any sea.

Which river crosses the Tropic of Cancer twice?

The Mahi — it is the only Indian river to cross the Tropic of Cancer twice. In Rajasthan it is dammed at Mahi Bajaj Sagar in Banswara.

Which dam on the Banas supplies drinking water to Jaipur?

The Bisalpur dam in Tonk district, which supplies drinking water to Jaipur, Ajmer and Tonk.
