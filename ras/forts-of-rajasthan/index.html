Forts of Rajasthan

In 2013, UNESCO inscribed six Hill Forts of Rajasthan as a World Heritage Site — Chittorgarh, Kumbhalgarh, Ranthambore, Amber (Amer), Jaisalmer and Gagron. Learn these six as one block; they are the single most repeated fact in this topic. Around them sit the great non-listed forts — Mehrangarh at Jodhpur, Junagarh at Bikaner, Jaigarh at Jaipur.

Chittorgarh and Kumbhalgarh — the Mewar giants

Chittorgarh is the largest fort in India by area and the emotional heart of Mewar. It carries the Vijay Stambh (Tower of Victory), raised by Rana Kumbha after defeating Mahmud Khilji, and the older Kirti Stambh (a Jain tower). Chittorgarh saw three jauhars. Kumbhalgarh, built by Rana Kumbha, has a boundary wall of roughly 36 km — among the longest walls in the world — and is the birthplace of Maharana Pratap.

  • Chittorgarh — largest fort in India by area; three jauhars
  • Vijay Stambh — built by Rana Kumbha after his victory over Mahmud Khilji
  • Kirti Stambh — a Jain tower, older than the Vijay Stambh
  • Kumbhalgarh — built by Rana Kumbha; wall ~36 km long
  • Kumbhalgarh is the birthplace of Maharana Pratap

Types of forts — durg classification

Ancient Indian texts classify forts by their natural defence, and RAS asks this directly. A giri-durg stands on a hill (Chittorgarh, Kumbhalgarh); a jal-durg is ringed by water (Gagron, surrounded by the Ahu and Kali Sindh rivers on three sides); a van-durg is shielded by forest (Ranthambore); a dhanva/maru-durg is protected by desert (Jaisalmer); and a parikha-durg is defended by a moat.

  • Giri-durg (hill fort) — Chittorgarh, Kumbhalgarh, Taragarh
  • Jal-durg (water fort) — Gagron (Jhalawar)
  • Van-durg (forest fort) — Ranthambore
  • Dhanva / Maru-durg (desert fort) — Jaisalmer
  • Parikha-durg (moat fort) — Bharatpur's Lohagarh, never conquered by the British

Mehrangarh, Junagarh and the other great forts

Not every famous fort is on the UNESCO list. Mehrangarh (Jodhpur) was founded by Rao Jodha in 1459 and towers over the blue city. Junagarh (Bikaner) was built by Raja Rai Singh and is unusual for standing on flat ground rather than a hill. Jaigarh (Jaipur) houses the Jaivana, once among the largest cannons on wheels in the world. Lohagarh (Bharatpur), a mud-walled moat fort, famously withstood repeated British assaults.

  • Mehrangarh (Jodhpur) — founded by Rao Jodha in 1459
  • Junagarh (Bikaner) — built by Raja Rai Singh; on flat ground, not a hill
  • Jaigarh (Jaipur) — houses the Jaivana cannon
  • Lohagarh (Bharatpur) — mud fort; never captured by the British
  • Taragarh (Ajmer) — 'star fort'; also a Taragarh at Bundi

How forts are asked in the RAS exam

  • Memorise the six UNESCO Hill Forts as one set (2013 inscription). A classic trick is to slip Mehrangarh or Junagarh into the list — neither is on it.
  • The durg classification is asked directly: Gagron is the jal-durg, Ranthambore the van-durg, Jaisalmer the maru-durg. These three pairings are near-guaranteed.
  • Pair fort with builder — Kumbhalgarh with Rana Kumbha, Mehrangarh with Rao Jodha, Amber with Raja Man Singh I, Junagarh with Raja Rai Singh.
  • Superlatives are safe scoring: Chittorgarh is India's largest fort by area, and Kumbhalgarh has one of the world's longest walls (~36 km).

Frequently asked questions

Which forts of Rajasthan are UNESCO World Heritage Sites?

Six Hill Forts, inscribed in 2013 — Chittorgarh, Kumbhalgarh, Ranthambore, Amber (Amer), Jaisalmer and Gagron.

Which is the largest fort in India?

Chittorgarh Fort in Rajasthan is the largest fort in India by area. It witnessed three jauhars and holds the Vijay Stambh.

Which fort is called a jal-durg (water fort)?

Gagron Fort in Jhalawar district — it is surrounded on three sides by the Ahu and Kali Sindh rivers.

Who built Kumbhalgarh Fort and why is it famous?

It was built by Rana Kumbha. It is famous for its roughly 36 km boundary wall — among the longest in the world — and as the birthplace of Maharana Pratap.

Which fort was never conquered by the British?

Lohagarh Fort in Bharatpur — a mud-walled moat fort that withstood repeated British assaults.
