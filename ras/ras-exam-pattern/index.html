RAS Exam Pattern 2026

The RPSC RAS exam has three stages — Preliminary, Main and Interview. The Prelims is only a screening test (its marks do not count in the final merit); the final merit is made from the Mains (800 marks) plus the Interview (100 marks) = 900 marks. Here is the full RAS 2026 exam pattern, paper by paper, with the marking scheme and negative marking.

RAS Prelims 2026 — pattern & negative marking

The RAS Preliminary examination is a single objective paper titled 'General Knowledge and General Science' — 150 questions for 200 marks, to be done in 3 hours. There is 1/3 negative marking for every wrong answer. The Prelims is purely a screening stage: you must clear it to reach the Mains, but its marks are not added to your final merit.

  • One paper: General Knowledge & General Science
  • 150 questions · 200 marks · 3 hours
  • 1/3 negative marking for each wrong answer
  • Qualifying stage only — marks not counted in the final merit
  • A fifth 'not attempted' option must be marked if you skip a question

RAS Mains 2026 — the four papers

Candidates who clear the Prelims sit the Main examination — four descriptive papers of 200 marks each (800 total), three hours per paper. Papers I–III are General Studies; Paper IV tests language and essay writing. Every paper counts towards the final merit.

  • Paper I — General Studies-I: History (Rajasthan/India/World), Economy, Sociology, Management, Accounts & Auditing — 200 marks
  • Paper II — General Studies-II: Administrative Ethics, Science & Technology, Earth Science (Geography & Geology) — 200 marks
  • Paper III — General Studies-III: Indian Polity, Governance & International Relations, Public Administration, Behaviour & Law — 200 marks
  • Paper IV — General Hindi (90) + General English (70) + Essay (40) — 200 marks
  • All four papers are descriptive, 3 hours each; no negative marking

Interview & how the final merit is made

The last stage is a personality test (interview) of 100 marks. The final merit list is prepared from the Main examination (800) plus the Interview (100) — a total of 900 marks. This is why the Mains, not the Prelims, decides your rank: a strong Prelims score buys you nothing beyond entry.

  • Interview / personality test: 100 marks
  • Final merit = Mains 800 + Interview 100 = 900 marks
  • Prelims marks do NOT count in the final merit
  • Prelims is used only to shortlist candidates for the Mains

What the pattern means for your strategy

  • Because Prelims marks are discarded, treat it as a threshold to cross — not a score to maximise. Clear it comfortably and move on.
  • Your rank is decided entirely by the Mains and Interview, so build descriptive answer-writing from day one instead of postponing it until after Prelims.
  • With 1/3 negative marking in Prelims, blind guessing hurts. Attempt only where you can eliminate at least two options.
  • Paper IV (Hindi, English & Essay) carries a full 200 marks — the same as any GS paper — yet is the most commonly neglected. It is the cheapest place to gain marks.

Frequently asked questions

How many stages are there in the RAS exam?

Three — Preliminary (objective screening), Main (four descriptive papers) and the Interview / personality test.

Is there negative marking in the RAS exam?

Yes, in the Prelims — 1/3 of a mark is deducted for every wrong answer. There is no negative marking in the descriptive Mains papers.

Are RAS Prelims marks counted in the final merit?

No. The Prelims is only a qualifying/screening stage. The final merit is made from the Mains (800 marks) and the Interview (100 marks) — 900 in total.

How many total marks is the RAS exam?

The final merit is out of 900 marks — Mains 800 plus Interview 100. The Prelims (200 marks) is separate and does not add to this.

How many papers are there in RAS Mains?

Four descriptive papers of 200 marks each — General Studies I, II and III, plus a fourth paper of General Hindi, General English and Essay.
